# 3. not yet

*lena — the first summer*

By the sixth week of Esme's first summer in Tuscany, Lena knew exactly what it meant when she started twisting the bottom of her shirt.

She was doing it now.

They had barely made it into the summer festival before Esme's fingers found the hem, rolling the fabric between them while she looked around the crowded square.

Six weeks ago, Lena probably wouldn't have noticed. Back then, Esme hardly talked unless somebody spoke to her first and spent most of her time looking like she was counting down the days until she could get back on a plane to Atlanta. By the second week, she was finishing Lena's sentences. Around week three, they started collecting inside jokes Clarke hated because neither of them would explain them. During week four, Esme turned twelve. Olivia made a cake, Aurelio sang loud enough for the whole valley, and Esme's dad called at eight that night, which was two in the afternoon in Atlanta, between lunch and dinner at his restaurant. The call lasted eleven minutes. Lena knew because she had been watching the kitchen clock and trying not to. Esme took the phone into the hallway, said *it's great, Daddy, everything's great* four times, and came back with her face very still. That night, Annemarie found them asleep on Lena's bedroom floor with three books, two flashlights, and a blanket they had somehow managed to pull halfway off the bed.

Now Lena knew the shirt thing meant Esme was nervous. She also knew Esme would deny it if she asked.

The summer festival was supposed to be one of the best nights on Lena's list. She had been talking about it since Esme arrived. The list was on the back page of her school diario, under Esme's flight number, which Lena had written at the top in May when Ginny read it to Annemarie over the phone, because it was the only fact about Esme anybody had given her. Underneath, in order, Lena had written gelato, the market, the bookshop, the library, the olive grove, and the festival. Four of them were already crossed off. She was saving the olive grove for last.

Strings of lights crossed above the narrow streets, and vendors had taken over almost every open space in the village. Tables were crowded with jewelry, scarves, sweets, handmade trinkets, and things Lena knew nobody actually needed but would buy anyway. Music played from speakers near the square, Britney Spears loud enough that Lena could make out "Oops!… I Did It Again" over the conversations around them.

Lena loved the festival every year, but this year Esme was here for it.

Clarke had stayed at Villa Reed with the adults because he apparently needed the evening light for whatever photography project had taken over his life that week. Lena suspected the crowd had more to do with it, but she didn't really care.

She had Esme to herself.

"Isn't this amazing?" Lena asked, grabbing Esme's arm near the entrance to the square. "I told you it was going to be good."

Esme glanced around at the crowded tables. "You've told me a lot of things."

"And I've been right about most of them," Lena replied.

"Debatable."

Lena started toward a table covered in little glass ornaments, then realized Esme hadn't followed. Her fingers had found the bottom of her shirt again.

"You okay?" Lena asked.

Esme nodded. "Yeah."

Lena stayed where she was and waited.

A few seconds passed before Esme sighed. "Everybody's talking really fast."

Lena looked around. Italian came from every direction, overlapping between vendors, families, and people calling to each other across the square. Lena never had to separate the words. To her, it just sounded like a Saturday.

"I can barely tell what anybody's saying," Esme added.

Lena made a face. Esme had gotten better since she arrived. The phrase book Ginny bought her was still shoved into her bag everywhere they went, but she didn't pull it out every five minutes anymore. Last week, she ordered her own gelato without Lena saying a word. A few days ago, she managed an entire conversation with one of Aurelio's vineyard workers before Ginny realized she wasn't needed.

"Besides, you're picking it up faster than you think," Lena said. "You ordered gelato by yourself."

Esme gave her a look. "I ordered chocolate."

"In Italian."

"It was one word."

"It counts," Lena insisted.

Esme smiled.

"You're half-Italian, remember?"

Her smile disappeared almost immediately. "Doesn't feel like it."

Lena opened her mouth, but somebody behind them spoke first.

"Turista."

Three boys from Lena's school were walking past. Emilo Tucci was in the middle, wearing a football jersey and the same stupid expression he always had when he thought he'd said something funny. He was thirteen now and had become completely unbearable about it.

Lena felt her face getting hot. Emilo had been looking directly at Esme.

"What did he say?" Esme asked.

"Nothing," Lena muttered.

One of the boys laughed.

Lena turned around. "Che cosa hai detto?" *What did you say?*

Emilo stopped walking and looked far too pleased with himself. "Ho detto che è una turista." *I said she's a tourist.*

Esme looked between them.

"Non è una turista," Lena shot back. *She's not a tourist.*

Emilo glanced at Esme again. "È americana." *She's American.*

"Sua madre è di qui." *Her mother is from here.*

"E allora?" *So?*

His friends laughed behind him.

Esme went quiet beside her, and Lena hated how Emilo looked at her. He kept doing it like he was examining something that didn't make sense to him.

"È stata qui tutta l'estate," Lena said. *She's been here all summer.*

Emilo shrugged. "Non significa che sia italiana." *That doesn't make her Italian.*

Heat climbed Lena's neck.

"Lei è più—" *She's more—*

Lena knew what she wanted to say. She could practically hear the sentence in her head, but suddenly there were too many words fighting to get out at once. Italian ones. English ones. Three different insults she knew would make Emilo angry and none of them arriving fast enough.

"Più italiana di…" *More Italian than…*

Emilo's mouth curled into a smile.

Something in Lena snapped.

"She's more Italian than you are!" Her voice came out louder in English than it had in Italian.

Esme tugged at her sleeve. "Lena."

Emilo understood her. Lena knew he did. Most of the kids at school spoke enough English to follow a simple conversation, and he'd spent half the year showing off every English phrase he learned whenever teachers were around.

His smirk got wider as Lena stepped closer.

"At least she remembers her nonna's birthday."

One of his friends laughed so hard he had to turn away.

Everybody at school knew Emilo had forgotten Signora Tucci's birthday in April because his grandmother complained about it to anyone who would listen for nearly a month.

"Sei proprio fastidiosa," he snapped. *You're so annoying.*

Lena's hands curled into fists. "Oh, stai zitto." *Oh, shut up.*

"Lena," Esme warned again.

Lena heard her, but Emilo still stood there, looking at Esme like she was the problem. Her ears felt hot now. Her heart had started pounding hard enough that she could hear it underneath the music coming from the square.

Emilo muttered something else about the American girl.

Lena took another step toward him. "E anche se lei fosse una—una…" *And even if she was a—a…*

She stopped again.

*Turista.*

Lena squeezed her eyes shut for half a second, furious with herself now too.

"Una turista," she finally got out. *A tourist.*

Emilo laughed.

Wrong choice.

Lena's eyes flew open.

"E allora? Almeno—almeno lei…" *So what? At least—at least she…*

Her hands were moving now because apparently the words couldn't do their job fast enough. She pointed at Esme, then at Emilo, trying to force the sentence into some kind of order.

"Lei sa essere gentile con le persone. Tu sei solo—sei…" *She knows how to be nice to people. You're just—you're…*

Nothing.

Lena stared at him.

She knew the word she wanted.

She knew at least five words she wanted.

Every single one disappeared.

Emilo raised his eyebrows. His friends were grinning. Lena could feel Esme holding onto the back of her shirt now, but even that wasn't enough to pull her out of it.

"Sei un… un…" *You're a… a…*

Her throat tightened.

She was so angry she could have screamed.

So she did.

"You're a JERK!"

The English ripped out of her loud enough that several people around them turned.

For half a second, the whole thing went quiet.

Then Emilo's friends started laughing again.

"Siete solo delle stupide," he muttered. *You're just stupid girls.*

Then he shoved one of his friends toward the other side of the square and walked away. Lena stared after him, still breathing too hard. Part of her wanted to follow, but Esme caught her hand before she could.

"Lena."

Lena looked down.

Her hands were shaking and she hadn't even noticed. She still had things she wanted to yell after him, half of them in Italian now that the words had decided to come back. Annemarie was always telling her to think before she reacted. Lena usually thought thinking first would ruin plenty of perfectly good reactions.

She could see her mother's point this time.

Maybe.

"You didn't have to do that," Esme said quietly.

Lena turned toward her. "Yes, I did."

Esme shook her head. "No, you didn't."

"He was being mean to you."

"People are mean." Esme looked down when she said it, like she was talking about the weather.

Lena frowned. "What does that mean?"

"Nothing."

"It doesn't sound like nothing."

Esme started twisting her shirt again. "It's just different at home."

Lena didn't say anything. She waited for Esme to keep going.

"In Atlanta, people usually don't say stuff like that out loud," Esme said. "They stare sometimes. Or they look at my mom and then at me like they're trying to figure out if she's actually my mother."

She touched one of her curls. "People ask weird questions about my hair too."

"What's wrong with your hair?" Lena asked.

Esme looked at her. "Nothing."

"Exactly."

A small smile appeared on Esme's face before she looked back toward the crowd.

"Sometimes people act surprised when I talk."

Lena frowned harder. "Why?"

Esme shrugged, but Lena had known her long enough now to recognize the difference between *I don't know* and *I don't want to explain it.*

"They expect something else."

Lena understood a little bit of what Esme meant. She'd watched adults ask Clarke about his "real parents" while Aurelio and Olivia were standing right beside him. Kids at school still made jokes about his accent even though most of them had known him for years. Girls in Lena's class had called her loud, dramatic, and too much more times than she could count.

Even while she was thinking it, though, Lena could feel it wasn't the whole thing. Nobody had ever looked at Lena and then at Annemarie to check. Nobody had ever been surprised when Lena talked, only sorry. She had just spent the whole fight with Emilo yelling that Esme was Italian, and Esme was telling her that being Italian wasn't what people were looking at. Lena hadn't been fighting for the wrong thing, exactly. Just not the whole thing. She didn't know yet what the rest of it was. She only knew she wanted to know it the next time.

She reached for Esme's hand. "People are stupid."

Esme laughed. "Very helpful."

Lena squeezed her hand. "If somebody says something like that again, tell me."

Esme glanced over. "So you can yell at them?"

"If I need to."

Esme shook her head, still smiling, before squeezing Lena's hand back. "Thanks. That's what best friends are for, right?"

Lena stared at her.

Best friends.

They had never actually called each other that before.

Clarke was Clarke. He had been around for as long as Lena could remember, and she loved him even when he annoyed the hell out of her. He was also her cousin. Family came with rules and holidays and people making you take pictures together even when you were mad.

Esme didn't have to like Lena.

She did anyway.

"Obviously," Lena said, because saying anything else suddenly felt embarrassing.

What she would have said, if it hadn't been embarrassing, was that nobody had ever picked her before. Girls at school picked her for relay teams because she was fast and for group projects because she would end up doing all the work. Nobody had ever picked Lena for Lena, loud and too much and all of it, and then said so out loud in the middle of the festival like it was something everybody already knew.

A stall across the square was covered in sparkly scarves, giving her the perfect excuse to move on.

"We need matching ones." Lena pulled Esme toward the table.

Esme took one look at the scarves. "No."

"Yes."

"My mother would never let me wear any of these."

"Then you definitely need one."

Esme picked up a purple scarf covered in beads and checked the handwritten tag. Silver glitter came off on her fingers. "Twenty-five thousand lire?"

"You have money," Lena said.

"I have *my mother's* money, which is supposed to last for the entire week."

"Same thing."

Esme looked offended. "It absolutely is not."

They didn't buy the scarves. Esme wiped the glitter off on her shorts and walked away without looking back. Lena looked back. She looked at the purple one for longer than she meant to, and on the way to the pastry stall she did some math about the birthday money in the tin on her bookshelf.

They spent the next hour doing almost everything else Lena wanted.

They ate pastries from a woman Annemarie knew and stood beside the fountain while Esme tried unsuccessfully to keep powdered sugar off her shirt. At one of the jewelry stalls, Esme asked the price of a bracelet in Italian and misunderstood the answer badly enough that she nearly handed the vendor twice what he wanted. Lena waited until they walked away to tell her.

"I hate you," Esme said. "You almost let me embarrass myself."

Lena shrugged. "It builds character."

Esme shoved her shoulder.

They tried on hats they had no intention of buying and invented stories about people walking through the square. Lena spotted a man near one of the food stalls with the largest mustache she had ever seen. "Oh my God."

Esme followed her eyes. "What?"

"He looks suspicious."

"He's buying cheese."

Esme stared at the man for another few seconds before lowering her voice. "Maybe he's undercover."

Lena turned toward her.

"Dangerous mozzarella situation," Esme added.

Lena laughed loudly enough that the man looked over at them.

Esme lost it first, and by the time they escaped to the other side of the square, they were both laughing so hard Lena's stomach hurt. The sun disappeared behind the buildings sometime after that, and the festival lights became brighter overhead. Esme stopped walking and tipped her head back to look up. For a few seconds, she seemed to forget whatever she'd been worried about earlier.

Lena wished Clarke had come.

He would have taken a picture.

Esme looked happy, and Lena wanted to keep it somewhere.

A moment later, Esme lowered her eyes toward the edge of the square. Ginny and Annemarie stood near one of the stalls, talking to somebody Lena didn't recognize, and the smile on Esme's face faded a little.

Lena knew why.

Esme talked about Atlanta most nights when they were supposed to be sleeping. Her dad's restaurant, where she was allowed to sit at the end of the counter and do homework if she stayed out of the way of the waiters. Her cousin Nia, who was thirteen and knew everything and told Esme so. Auntie Saundra's house on the Fourth of July, which Esme had missed for the first time in her life two weeks into the summer and talked about for three nights straight like it had died. Places Lena had never seen and people she would probably never meet.

Her real life was still waiting for her.

Six weeks had already gone by, and Lena suddenly hated the number.

Esme would leave when summer ended.

Atlanta was farther away than anywhere Lena cared about. Clarke went to England and came back all the time. England belonged to part of his life here.

Atlanta was different.

Atlanta meant school starting again without Lena. Phone calls that cost too much for them to make whenever they wanted. Letters that would take days to arrive. Esme going back to friends who had known her before Lena knew she existed. The internet at the villa was so slow Aurelio yelled at the computer whenever somebody tried to use it while he was on the phone.

Lena had no idea how a best friendship was supposed to work across an ocean.

"What?" Esme asked.

Lena realized she had been staring. "Nothing."

"You're being weird."

"I'm never weird," Lena said.

Esme gave her a look.

Lena turned toward the pastry stalls before she had to explain herself. August wasn't here yet.

They still had time for the olive grove, which Lena had saved for last on purpose, and two more Saturdays of the market. Lena still had Italian to teach her. Esme still refused to try pistachio gelato because she said green ice cream looked suspicious, and Lena considered fixing that a personal responsibility.

A lot could happen before Esme had to go home.

Maybe she'd love Italy enough to miss it.

Maybe she'd convince Ginny they needed to come back next summer.

Lena really, really wanted there to be a next summer.

She pointed toward a pastry stand before Esme could ask what was wrong again. "I bet I can eat more cannoli than you."

Esme stared at her. "You almost threw up after three last week."

"Unrelated."

"It absolutely was not."

Lena started walking faster.

Esme caught up beside her, grinning. "Oh, you're on, Cavalcanti."

They took off through the crowd, and Lena let August stay where it belonged—weeks away. She still had Esme now, and she wasn't ready to think about goodbye.

At least, not yet.
