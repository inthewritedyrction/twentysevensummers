# 5. happy birthday, walker

*esme — the sixth summer*

"Esmerelda, vieni qui. Signor Moretti vuole conoscerti." *Esmerelda, come here. Signor Moretti wants to meet you.*

Esme had been seventeen for approximately eleven hours, and Nonna Franca had already introduced her to three people she was apparently expected to remember for the rest of her life. She crossed the courtyard anyway, smoothing one hand over the dark green silk at her hip before her grandmother could do it for her.

Galleria Amatto looked completely different after dark. The tall doors facing the courtyard had been thrown open, carrying music through rooms usually quiet enough to force their visitors to whisper. Gold lights disappeared into the jasmine climbing the stone walls outside, and white roses spilled from low arrangements between bottles of wine and little cards asking guests to use the disposable cameras scattered across the tables. Waiters slipped through the crowd with crostini, fried zucchini blossoms, and pecorino drizzled with honey while relatives kept shifting chairs around every table Ginevra had spent the afternoon arranging.

The guest list explained why her mother had called this birthday important for the last two months.

Seventeen in Atlanta meant senior year, college applications, and her dad asking every few weeks whether Esme had finally decided where she wanted to apply. Seventeen in the Amatto family had apparently inspired her grandmother to invite gallery patrons.

Franca touched Esme's elbow as she approached. "Signor Moretti, questa è mia nipote, Esmerelda Amatto." *This is my granddaughter, Esmerelda Amatto.*

Moretti greeted Esme warmly enough that she almost forgave the last name. He owned a collection in Milan that Ginevra had discussed over breakfast for half the summer, and Esme remembered enough about an upcoming exhibition to ask the right question. His answer came quickly in Italian. Esme followed without translating anything in her head first, asked another question when he finished and caught the pleased smile her mother tried to hide behind her wineglass.

Five summers ago, she had needed a phrase book to order gelato.

Tonight, Signor Moretti never once switched to English.

He eventually asked about school. Esme told him senior year started in August and that she would be applying to universities in the fall. Before she could change the subject, Moretti turned toward Ginevra.

"E dopo l'università, forse una terza generazione qui?" *And after university, perhaps a third generation here?*

Ginevra's hand settled between Esme's shoulder blades. "Vedremo." *We'll see.*

Moretti smiled as though something had already been agreed upon.

Esme smiled too because six summers of Amatto functions had taught her exactly how to do that.

Once he moved away, she snatched a zucchini blossom from the nearest tray and bit into it before her mother could introduce her to anyone else.

"You're going to get crumbs on your dress."

"I'm starving."

"You ate."

Esme rolled her eyes before stuffing her face. "At five."

Ginevra plucked something invisible from Esme's shoulder anyway. Her own gold dress caught the lights every time she moved, and she had spent most of the evening moving. A gallery opening, dinner, or birthday made very little difference once Ginevra Amatto decided to host something. The birthday had only given her another excuse to show everyone just how *perfect* being an Amatto was.

"You were wonderful with Moretti," she said.

"I've been doing that for years." Esme shrugged, still chewing. "He asks me the same thing every year."

"Yes, but now you sound comfortable."

Esme took another bite before her mother could see how much she liked hearing it. Ginevra caught the smile anyway and kissed the side of her head, careful of the hair she had spent most of the afternoon having straightened.

"Stay near me after dinner. The Bellinis are coming."

"Mom," Esme sighed.

"One night, Esme." Ginevra shot her a look.

"I have been performing for your friends since seven."

"You have been eating half the hors d'oeuvres since seven."

Esme laughed around the last bite. "I can do both."

Ginevra smiled before somebody called her name from inside the gallery and pulled her away.

Esme exhaled and reached for another zucchini blossom.

"Girl, if you don't turn around."

She knew the voice immediately.

Nia Walker crossed the courtyard in a bright orange dress, her braids piled high and gold hoops swinging as she opened both arms. Esme abandoned the food and met her halfway. Nia squeezed her hard enough to wrinkle something Ginevra would probably notice later. "Seventeen! Let me see you."

"Please don't start."

"I haven't seen you since May. I'm allowed to start." Nia stepped back, taking in the dress and the straight hair with a grin. "Aunt Ginny got you in here looking expensive as hell."

Esme laughed. "You flew to Italy to call me expensive?"

"I flew to Italy because Uncle Michael paid for the ticket."

Nia was eighteen and starting at Spelman in August, a fact she had mentioned at every Walker family event since getting accepted. She had been talking about dorm shopping before Esme left Atlanta in June and apparently had not stopped, judging by the Spelman keychain hanging from the tiny purse at her wrist. When Esme was seven, Nia had been the one to tell her the truth about the tooth fairy. Esme had never quite forgiven her for it, and had never once stopped believing the next thing Nia told her.

Jordan reached them next, already tugging at a tie he had probably been wearing for less than twenty minutes. He and Esme were both heading into senior year, although Jordan had responded to every college question that summer with some variation of *I'll figure it out*.

He hugged Esme and immediately accused Ginevra of making her look grown.

"You saw me two months ago."

"Different country." Jordan shook his head, giving up on the tie altogether. "Italy brings out the worst in our parents, I swear."

"Tell me about it," Esme replied. "Is Auntie Saundra here?"

It was Nia's turn to shake her head. "Nah. Stayed home with Kiyomi. Didn't want to attempt international travel with a three-year-old."

"Fair."

Micah caught up carrying three gift bags and the expression of a fifteen-year-old boy who was just glad to be in the space. "Can somebody please take one of these?"

Esme took the smallest one as Micah muttered under his breath while Nia laughed and shoved another bag into Jordan's chest. Their noise carried across the courtyard, causing several Amatto heads to turn.

Then Michael Walker came through the gallery doors.

Her father had exchanged his chef coat for the black suit he wore whenever a restaurant opening required him to act like he enjoyed formal events. His tie was already slightly crooked, and Esme could tell he had been pulling at the knot during the drive over.

She forgot everybody else for a second.

The last time her father had been in the room for her birthday, she had been eleven. He had made a lemon pound cake at six in the morning so he could still be at the restaurant by ten, and she had eaten the first slice for breakfast at the kitchen counter in her pajamas while her mother told them both it was ridiculous and ate a slice too. Every birthday since had been a phone call. He timed them around the six hours and the restaurant, so they always reached her in the middle of something on this side of the ocean: a toast, a cake coming out of the kitchen, Nonna Franca telling her to stand up straight. Twelve had been a phone call. So had thirteen, fourteen, fifteen and sixteen. Esme had gotten very good at carrying the phone into a hallway and telling him everything was great.

Michael opened his arms and Esme ran straight for them. He hugged her tightly and rocked her once before setting her back far enough to see her face. "Seventeen."

"You're acting like I turned forty."

"Feels about the same from here." His smile made hers come easier. Michael kissed her forehead. "Happy birthday, baby girl. You look beautiful."

No hand went to her hair. He didn't straighten the front of her dress or remind her to stand differently. He kissed her again, then walked away. Nia made a noise behind them.

Esme twisted around. "Mind your business."

"I am." Nia's eyes moved past Esme. "But he's not."

Michael had just greeted Aurelio near the door, and Ginevra was crossing the gallery toward them.

Esme understood immediately. "Oh God."

Ginevra reached Michael before the conversation could get worse. She kissed both his cheeks, asked about the flight, and complained that he had refused the car she offered to send. Michael reminded her he had been traveling internationally since before Esme was born and could manage an airport without Amatto assistance.

Five years of divorce had improved them tremendously.

Esme still wouldn't have left them alone with a bottle of wine and unresolved feelings.

Nonna Franca arrived a minute later. Michael's shoulders straightened instantly, the way Esme's did whenever Nonna was in the room.

"Michael," Franca said.

"Franca."

"Your flight was comfortable?"

"Very."

"I'm glad."

Nia leaned closer to Esme. "She still scare everybody?"

Jordan answered for her. "Everybody except our mother."

"I think she scares your mother too," Esme replied. "Auntie Saundra just never could hold her tongue when it came to Nonna."

Franca's head turned slightly before she waved over at them.

Nia smiled at her.

Esme almost choked.

Dinner came an hour or so later.

Ginevra had spread guests between the gallery and courtyard instead of forcing everyone into one enormous formal seating arrangement. The Amattos immediately began rearranging chairs anyway. Michael ended up at a table with Aurelio, Olivia and two of Ginevra's relatives. Nia somehow landed beside Giuliana and Valentina. Jordan spent ten minutes trying to convince Ellio that Atlanta had better nightlife than Florence despite never having been legally old enough to experience either.

Esme was just grateful that the party seemed to loosen up as people ate.

English started mixing into the Italian around Esme instead of replacing it. Michael and Aurelio argued about greens. Aurelio held that anything cooked longer than ten minutes had given up on being a vegetable, and Michael, whose collards sat on the back of the stove all afternoon with a smoked turkey wing, treated this as a personal attack. Olivia wanted the recipe for the peach cobbler Esme had been describing at Villa Reed for five summers. Zia Lucia asked Nia about Spelman and actually listened long enough for Nia to explain why she chose it. Micah discovered that Valentina liked the same video game he did and abandoned his earlier claim that the entire evening was boring. Valentina promised to play it with him before they left at the end of the week.

Esme moved between all of them.

She translated something for Micah when one of the older relatives spoke too quickly, then turned around and answered Lucia in Italian without thinking. Jordan rescued her from a conversation with a gallery donor by appearing with a plate and announcing that she hadn't eaten enough. Ginevra realized exactly what he was doing but let him get away with it.

It went on like that for most of dinner, and once Esme noticed herself doing it, she couldn't stop noticing. She laughed at her father's jokes half a second before the Amatto table understood them and at Aurelio's half a second before the Walker table did. She knew to kiss Zia Lucia on both cheeks and to hug Nia hard enough to hurt. She told Giuliana what a cookout was and told Micah that nobody here would think about dessert before ten. She knew which of her cousins needed rescuing and which of them would be insulted to be rescued, on both sides of the courtyard. Nobody else at the party could do all of it. Esme had spent six summers carrying each family's rules back and forth across the ocean in the same suitcase. Tonight she had to unpack both in one courtyard at the same time. It was the most tiring thing she had ever been good at.

She was a little proud of it anyway.

It was the first time Esme remembered having both families in the same place without a holiday, funeral or divorce being responsible.

A camera flashed from somewhere near the gallery doors, and Esme turned quickly, hoping that whoever it was had at least caught her good side.

She was expecting one of her mother's photographers.

Instead, *he* was there.

Three days ago, Clarke's Canon had been attached to him while she stood barefoot on a Tuscan road with a dead Ferragamo in her hand.

Tonight, his camera sat against a white shirt beneath a dark jacket, and the curls that had been touching the back of his neck were suddenly gone.

He had cut his hair.

Nia followed her attention, took in Clarke, and returned to her conversation with Giuliana without a word. Esme bumped her knee underneath the table, and Nia smiled into her glass.

Clarke was standing with Aurelio and one of the gallery curators. The woman had known him since he was thirteen, but instead of asking about his photography or commenting on how much he had grown, she asked what he would be studying that fall.

"Storia e tutela dei beni artistici," *History and Conservation of Artistic Heritage,* Clarke answered.

The curator asked something about his first year at the University of Florence. Clarke said he was heading there in a few weeks and that he was excited, in the polite voice he used when he wanted an adult to finish with him.

Esme pressed the edge of her napkin beneath her thumb.

She had known he was applying to things. Everybody had known. It came up the way weather came up, and every time Aurelio mentioned it, Clarke suddenly needed to be somewhere else. Four weeks she had been back. They had sat in a café for an hour waiting out the rain. He had carried her home up a hill. He had told her about his name. He had not told her he'd chosen a degree, or a city, or that the city was close enough to Villa Reed for Sunday dinners and an ocean away from Atlanta. She had to find out standing beside a curator who called it a very sensible choice.

She hadn't asked, she reminded herself. That was fair.

It didn't help.

Ginevra had asked. Esme knew because fifteen minutes later, she brought Signora Bellini to their table.

"Esme begins her final year in August," her mother said after the introductions. "She'll be applying to universities this fall."

Bellini brightened. "Here in Florence?"

"I'm applying to schools in Atlanta too," Esme said, smiling politely.

"Of course." The woman patted Esme's hand. "Although with the gallery here, I imagine the choice will be easy."

Esme's mouth opened.

Ginevra answered first. "We'll see."

Across the courtyard, Clarke was still talking about the degree he had chosen. Florence, then, from both directions at once. Her mother's version had the gallery in it and a third generation and Esme's name on an office door. Clarke's version didn't have Esme in it at all, which was either the whole problem or no problem whatsoever, and she did not want to find out which in front of Signora Bellini.

Jordan caught her expression and changed the subject by asking Signora Bellini a question about a painting near the entrance. Esme would forgive him for being annoying until at least Christmas.

Behind them, someone replaced Ginevra's selected background music with "La Tortura," and Valentina immediately dragged Giuliana into the courtyard. A few songs later, the Black Eyed Peas came through the speakers, and whatever distance had remained between the younger Walkers and Amattos disappeared.

Nia kicked off her sandals.

"Absolutely not," Esme said when her cousin reached for her hand.

Nia kept reaching.

"These heels are new," Esme whined.

"Then don't break them." Nia chuckled.

"My mother is standing right there." Esme glanced toward Ginevra, who was too busy talking to Clarke's mom to notice.

"Good. She can see you having fun at your own birthday."

Esme shook her head but grabbed her cousin's hand. Jordan and Ellio had already made fools of themselves in the center of the courtyard. Micah tried to join them, and both boys immediately told him he had no rhythm. Valentina defended him long enough for Micah to become visibly interested in whatever Valentina said next.

Lena arrived while Esme was still laughing. She crossed the courtyard in a pale blue dress without so much as a cough to ruin her perfectly done makeup.

"You had us hiking across Tuscany for medicine." Esme met her near the doors. "You are a liar."

"I was sick." Lena hugged her and pressed a wrapped gift into her hands. "Happy birthday."

Lena joined the others almost immediately and somehow fit into the Walker cousins' energy without needing an introduction beyond her name. Ten minutes later, Nia had an arm around her while they shouted the same part of a song. Jordan and Lena argued about whether Esme had been more annoying at twelve or sixteen. Esme heard enough of the conversation to threaten them both.

Her mother's chosen hairstyle began losing the fight sometime after nine.

The front pieces curled first.

Nia saw one forming near Esme's temple and reached toward it.

"Don't."

"I wasn't fixing it." Nia pulled it so it sprang more. "I like it."

Esme's shoulders dropped.

Across the courtyard, Ginevra noticed the same curl. Her hand started to rise automatically before Michael stepped beside her and offered her one of the little pastries from the dessert table. Her dad winked at Esme, then turned back to her mother and continued their conversation.

Thirty minutes later, the cake arrived.

Ginevra had ordered millefoglie layered with pastry cream and strawberries, with seventeen narrow gold candles crowded across the top. Everybody gathered around the long courtyard table while staff dimmed the nearest lights.

Michael came to Esme's right.

Ginevra took the left.

For five summers, her parents had been on opposite ends of a phone line and an ocean, and the closest they came to standing together was the same question asked twice a year, once in each country: *Do you have your passport?* Now she had one of them on each side of her and the candles going in front of her, and for one second, having them both there felt more significant than the cake.

Then Nia started singing "Happy Birthday" before anybody else was ready.

Ginevra laughed and answered in Italian.

The Amatto side joined her.

The Walker cousins got louder.

By the time everybody reached the end, two different birthday songs were happening in two languages at two different speeds. Michael had stopped singing because he was laughing. Olivia was trying to follow both. Lena gave up entirely and clapped along.

Clarke stood behind the crowd, camera raised.

Esme blew out all seventeen candles.

The courtyard erupted when her mother kissed one cheek and Michael kissed the other.

Nia wrapped both arms around her from behind while Micah announced that seventeen was ancient and Jordan reminded him that his own fifteenth birthday had been four months ago.

Zia Lucia kissed Esme and called her *Esmerelda Amatto.*

Nia immediately called her E.

Nonna Franca told her she was proud of the young woman she was becoming, then smoothed the same curl Ginevra had resisted fixing earlier.

By eleven, the function Ginevra planned had mostly disappeared.

White napkins sat crumpled beside half-empty glasses. Somebody had moved two of the flower arrangements to the ground to make room for elbows on the table. Micah's jacket hung over the back of a chair. Nia had been barefoot for nearly an hour. Giuliana had abandoned whatever elegant posture Franca taught her and was sitting sideways in Ellio's chair while arguing with Lena about music.

The gallery still belonged to the Amattos.

The party no longer belonged to any one side of the family.

Esme slipped upstairs while everyone was distracted.

The hallway outside Ginevra's office was quiet enough that the bass from downstairs traveled through the floor instead of the walls. She carried her shoes in one hand and pushed open the French doors leading to the small balcony above the courtyard.

Esme leaned against the stone railing and watched her family. Below, Michael and Aurelio were laughing at something near the gallery entrance. Olivia stood with Ginevra and one of Esme's aunts. Nia danced with Lena while Jordan attempted to take a picture of them with one of the disposable cameras. Micah had somehow gotten Valentina to sit beside him.

The scene made absolutely no sense.

"You cut it," she said, as the balcony door opened behind her.

Clarke touched the back of his hair. "Somebody kept complaining."

He came beside her and rested his forearms on the railing.

For a while, they watched the party, until Aurelio said something downstairs that sent Michael into another fit of laughter.

"Nobody warned me our dads would get along this well," Esme said.

"They've had decades of knowing each other," Clarke replied. "From what I've gathered, they were friends when they were younger."

"Friends?" Esme raised an eyebrow. "Michael and Aurelio?"

Clarke nodded. "At least that's what Lena's mom says."

Esme looked back down at her father. He had his head tipped back, laughing the way he laughed in the restaurant kitchen after the last ticket went out, and Aurelio had one hand on his shoulder like it had been there before. For six summers she had assumed her father knew Aurelio the way he knew everything about Italy, secondhand, through her. Apparently he had a history with this man that started before she did, and nobody had thought to mention it. Esme filed that away with the other things she was going to ask about later, which was becoming a long list.

"Hm," she said. "So it won't be weird when he invites him to Atlanta?"

Clarke laughed softly before moving the package from beneath his arm. "I made you something."

Esme accepted the brown paper parcel. There was no ribbon or bow on it. She glanced up at Clarke and opened it.

A dark blue photo album rested inside.

Esme ran her fingers across the cover before opening to the first page.

Eleven-year-old Esme sat beside the pool at Villa Reed with her feet in the water and her phrase book open across her knees. Her hair was everywhere from the flight. Her face was turned toward Lena instead of the camera.

The first day.

"You did take it."

Clarke leaned against the railing beside her. "Yeah."

Six summers. He had been sitting on it for six summers, and she had stood next to him through all of them without knowing.

Summer Two showed Lena asleep across Esme's legs, with books covering half the bed around them. Another photograph showed Esme and Lena at the market with bags on both arms and no idea Clarke had fallen several feet behind.

Summer Three had Esme covered in flour.

She remembered that disaster.

Aurelio tried teaching them to bake something none of them had any business attempting, and Lena had knocked an entire bowl onto Esme before blaming gravity. Summer Four filled several pages. The villa. The library. Esme reading barefoot beneath an olive tree. Clarke had caught her asleep once with a book covering half her face.

Summer Five showed more versions of her than Esme remembered existing.

Straight hair at an Amatto dinner.

Curls the next morning.

Esme in an old T-shirt beside Lena.

Esme dressed for church with Ginevra.

Esme chewing something in Annemarie's kitchen with her mouth embarrassingly full.

She stayed on those two pages longer than the others. She had always thought of them as separate girls, more or less. The one her mother dressed for dinners. The one who woke up the next morning and let her hair do what it wanted. The one Lena got, and the one who sat through Mass beside Ginevra and kept her opinions to herself. Esme had spent five summers switching between them so fast she had stopped noticing the switch. She had never seen them all lined up in one place before. None of them looked like a mistake. None of them looked like a costume, either. They just looked like her, over and over, on different days.

Then she reached the last picture on the page and started laughing. "Why is this in here?"

Clarke checked the photograph. "You asked me for a copy."

"I did not."

"You did."

The memory came back slowly.

She absolutely had.

Esme kept turning pages. This summer appeared near the end. Lena stretched across the kitchen table. Aurelio asleep in a chair outside. Esme between shelves in the gallery archives with a pencil tucked behind her ear.

Then the café.

Rain covered the window behind her while Esme sat with her chin in one hand, attention somewhere beyond the glass.

She couldn't remember Clarke lifting the camera. "You kept this all this time?"

"I keep everything." Clarke shrugged.

Esme turned back through the album from the end to the beginning, slower this time. Lena. Aurelio. Olivia on the kitchen counter. Annemarie with a dish towel over her shoulder. Esme, Esme, Esme. Six summers of Villa Reed, and Clarke wasn't in a single one of them.

Of course he wasn't. He had been on the other side of every picture, deciding what went in.

Esme closed the album and hugged it lightly against her stomach. "Thank you."

Clarke rubbed his thumb along the edge of his camera strap. The tips of his ears were turning pink again. "Happy birthday, Walker."

"Thanks, Reed."

Clarke leaned toward her before she understood what he was doing.

His lips touched her cheek for barely a second, but it was long enough for Esme to notice her heart was beating fast as hell.

One cheek.

Half of Tuscany had kissed Esme hello and goodbye for six summers. Aurelio and Olivia every June. Zia Lucia, Nonna Franca, the woman at the bakery in town who had decided by the second summer that Esme was family. Clarke too, at the start of every summer and the end of it, quick and automatic in the doorway, left and then right, the same way he greeted everybody. Esme knew exactly what that kiss felt like. She could have done it in her sleep.

This wasn't that. Nobody kissed one cheek and stopped. Nobody did it without hello or goodbye attached. Nobody did it on a balcony where nobody else was watching.

Clarke's ears were fully red now. Esme found herself fighting the urge to touch the spot where he'd kissed her.

"ESME!" Nia's voice carried up from the courtyard before either of them could make the next move.

She moved to the railing.

Nia stood below beside Jordan with a camera raised over her head. Half the younger cousins were gathered around her.

"We need everybody!"

Esme laughed. "You have everybody."

"CLARKE TOO!"

Well.

Apparently, Nia had figured out more than Esme hoped.

Clarke picked up his camera.

Esme caught his sleeve before he reached the door. "No."

He paused.

"You're getting in this one."

Something shifted in his face before he nodded.

Downstairs, Nia reorganized the entire courtyard for the photograph. Michael ended up on Esme's right and Ginevra on her left. Franca stood beside her daughter, while Olivia and Aurelio came in from the other side. Lena squeezed between Jordan and Giuliana, and Micah complained about somebody standing on his shoe until Valentina told him to move over.

Clarke started to step back with his camera, but Nia caught the strap before he got very far.

"You too."

He frowned. "Who's taking it?"

Nia handed the PowerShot to a waiter. "Problem solved."

Clarke gave in and joined the group beside Esme. His shoulder settled against hers as everyone tried to fit into the frame, Aurelio calling for somebody in the back to move left while Michael complained that Jordan was blocking half his face.

The first flash caught everybody the second they were ready.

Jordan ruined the second one.

Whatever he said sent Michael into laughter. Ginevra turned toward Esme, Franca kept talking, and Lena's eyes closed at the wrong time. Clarke laughed beside her, and Esme turned toward him without thinking.

The waiter took a third for insurance before handing Clarke his camera back.

"Second one is probably terrible," Nia said, immediately reaching for it.

Clarke flipped through the pictures on the screen and handed the camera over. Nia laughed at the first bad one, then went quiet on the next.

Esme leaned closer. "What?"

Nia angled the screen toward her.

The picture was terrible.

Michael wasn't looking at the camera. Lena's eyes were shut. Micah had been caught halfway through saying something, and Ginevra was turned almost completely sideways.

Esme barely noticed any of them, because right in the center, she noticed it.

She was looking at Clarke.

Her head was tipped toward him, the smile on her face nothing like the polite one she had used with Moretti or the big one she gave her father when he arrived.

Nia glanced from the camera to Esme.

Esme kept staring at the screen.

She was seventeen, not twelve. Girls at school had spent years talking about who kissed who after football games and which boy somebody swore they'd never speak to again before showing up Monday wearing his jacket. Esme had seen that look on other people's faces a hundred times.

Esme had just never seen it on her own face before.

Especially not like that.

"Oh," she said.

For once, Nia didn't make the joke sitting right there waiting for her. She only bumped her shoulder against Esme's before handing the camera back to Clarke. Jordan dragged Nia toward the courtyard before she could protest. Lena joined them almost immediately, and within minutes the photograph disappeared into the rest of the party.

Esme stayed where she was for another second.

She would end up with a copy of that picture. She never could remember asking for it.

Clarke had moved a few feet away to take another picture. He caught her staring and smiled before raising the camera.

Three days ago, Esme would have complained.

Tonight, she smiled back.
